Where these numbers come from. I pulled live data from EA's Pro Clubs feed on 30 Sep 2026: 375 recent league matches from the 40 highest-ranked FC 27 clubs, which gave 4,764 player-match lines (5,038 before filtering). Your club's numbers will differ, and the app re-learns from your own matches once enough are logged.

2 · The hidden event counters

Each player line carries up to four match_event_aggregate strings such as 214:1,217:3,216:4. EA doesn't document them. Across the corpus there are 229 distinct codes. To decode them, I tested each code, and sums of codes, against the stats we already know.

The strongest matches check out: code 214 equals goals in 99% of player-matches, and codes 217 + 218 add up to total shots 97% of the time. Anything below "high" is an inference from correlations, so treat it as a lead rather than a fact.

The top bar in each card, explained, is the share of the rating these stats account for. The rest comes from things EA doesn't itemise, such as positioning, interceptions and dribbles.

Small bars that point the "wrong" way (for example, completed passes slightly negative for forwards) come from overlap between stats, since players who pass a lot also score less. Don't read anything into bars under ±0.1.

6 · How the stats interact

A stat on its own rarely tells the story. These pairings are what the app uses to separate a good performance from one that only looks good on paper.

Volume × accuracy (passing)

For midfielders, a misplaced pass costs about 2.6 times what a completed pass earns (−0.034 vs +0.013). That puts the break-even at about 72% accuracy. Above it, more passing raises the rating. Below it, every extra pass lowers it.

break-even accuracy = miss cost ÷ (miss cost + completion gain) = 0.034 ÷ (0.034 + 0.013) ≈ 72%

This gives four passing profiles:

  • High volume, high accuracy: the team's hub. Protect it.
  • Low volume, high accuracy: the player is hiding. They're safe but not involved, so tell them to show for the ball.
  • High volume, low accuracy: forcing passes. Simplify.
  • Low volume, low accuracy: out of the game. Look at their positioning first.

The shooting funnel

Shots → on target → goals. Across the corpus, 72% of shots hit the target and 52% of on-target shots were scored. Where the funnel leaks tells you what to coach:

  • Few shots: movement and getting into the box.
  • Many shots, low on-target rate: shot selection and technique (shooting under pressure, from bad angles).
  • On target but rarely scoring: placement. The keeper is getting to them, so aim for corners and use finesse or low driven shots.

Tackle attempts × success

For midfielders a won tackle is worth +0.20 and a missed one −0.03, so engaging pays for itself in rating terms at just 12% success. Defenders break even at 11%. Rating therefore rewards aggression. The team cost of a missed tackle, a runner in behind, doesn't show up in the missing player's rating. It shows up as goalsconceded for everyone. The app flags high volume with low success (below ~30%) as a timing problem even when the player's rating looks fine.

Saves × goals conceded (goalkeepers)

A goal conceded costs 0.41 and a save earns 0.16, so a keeper needs about 2.5 saves to cancel out one goal. That is why keeper ratings run ~1.3 points below outfield players. Many saves plus many goals against points at the defence rather than the keeper. Few saves with many goals against points at the keeper.

Individual stats × result

Winning adds about +0.2 to every outfield player's rating. Compare ratings within a result (wins against wins) before drawing conclusions about a player.

What the feed can't see

The stats explain 77% of a forward's rating, 74% of a keeper's, 50% of a midfielder's and only 34% of a defender's. When a defender's rating is well above or below what their stats predict, the difference comes from positioning, interceptions and blocks. The app shows that gap as "Everything else" and treats a consistently negative gap as an off-the-ball positioning theme.

Out-shooting the opponent is by far the strongest team signal. Winning the tackle battle comes next. Pass accuracy matters least of the three. For club-level suggestions, the app asks two questions: are we creating more shots than we allow, and are we winning our duels?

9 · From stats to suggestions

  1. Normalise. Scale counting stats to 90 minutes, drop players under 20 minutes, and flag anyone with a high idle share.
  2. Compare by position. Place each metric against that position's percentiles (P25 = flag, P75 = strength) and against the player's own rolling average.
  3. Read the pairs, not single stats. Apply the interaction rules below, which turn "62% passing" into a specific diagnosis.
  4. Attribute the rating. Break the rating down with the per-action weights, then show the unexplained remainder as "Everything else".
  5. Attach coaching. Each diagnosis maps to position-specific best-practice tips.
  6. Promote to themes. A diagnosis that appears in 30%+ of a player's 8+ matches becomes an overarching theme, tagged improving, steady or worsening by comparing the last 5 matches with earlier ones.
